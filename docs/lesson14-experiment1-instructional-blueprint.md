# Lesson 14, Experiment 1 — Instructional blueprint

**Status:** design for review; no application implementation is specified as complete. The Stage 1 apparatus and physics are the starting point. This experience teaches the point-charge case and the choice of origin before students tackle the other Lesson 14 source distributions.

The guide and workbook cover five source geometries, symmetry, normalization of $dQ$, and superposition. Experiment 1 uses **one discrete charge** to establish the source/observation language and exterior method. Workbook Activity 1b–1c and Activities 3–7 remain optional links or later experiments; this scene must not imply that its origin-dependent $Q_1$ is a physical second charge or that it teaches a two-charge dipole. The HTML lab uses normalized potential units for cross-distribution comparisons; the VR wand uses **volts** for its $+1\,\mathrm{nC}$ source and must label them as such.

### Likely conceptual difficulties to address

1. Treating potential as a direction-bearing field or interpreting amber surfaces as field lines. Potential is a scalar value at a point; for this positive point charge the electric field points radially outward and is related to how $V$ changes in space. Equal potential does not mean zero field.
2. Confusing the source with its coordinate $\mathbf r'$, the observation tip with its coordinate $\mathbf r$, or the prime mark with a derivative. Both vectors start at the **chosen** $O$; the physical separation is the vector difference and does not depend on $O$.
3. Treating $Q_n$ as additional charge pieces. $Q_0$ is total charge; higher moments are signed, position-weighted descriptions. $Q_n$ has units $\mathrm{C}\,\mathrm{m}^{n}$, so different $Q_n$ must not share an unlabeled bar scale.
4. Assuming the centered source's undefined $\theta'$ makes its higher moments undefined. The factor $r'^n P_n(\cos\theta')$ tends to zero for $n\ge1$, since $|P_n|\le1$.
5. Mistaking the convergence boundary for a physical shell, a zero-potential surface, or a wall. It is centered on $O$, changes with $O$, and marks where the **exterior series** has a guarantee; exact Coulomb potential remains available away from the charge.
6. Believing a finite truncation must equal the exact value everywhere outside the boundary. Each finite sum omits a remainder; agreement generally improves at larger $r/R_{\max}$ and with more terms, but individual term signs and local error need not improve monotonically at every order.

## Common interaction and teaching contract

- **One simulation, two presentations.** The physical charge is fixed. The Quest near grab and ray grab move the wand by its grip and the origin carriage by its grip; the wand's **tip** is the observation point. The origin slides only along the polar axis. Locomotion, snap turn, recenter, reset, and Exit VR remain available throughout. Guided and Free Exploration never use different physics or disable grabbing.
- **The instructional loop is predict → manipulate → observe → explain → apply.** A prediction is an invitation, never an entry gate. The student may manipulate first, revisit a prediction, repeat a phase, jump ahead, or switch modes. Guidance follows the current physical state; a phase change only changes prompts and overlay visibility. If a student explores a later idea early, show a brief contextual hint and an optional “explain this now” link.
- **Evidence before answer.** On an incorrect prediction, do not mark the student wrong immediately. Ask for a comparison that separates competing explanations, highlight the relevant two points or vector endpoints, and offer a second attempt. A “show reasoning” option reveals the explanation on request. Record a changed prediction as learning, not as failure.
- **Small local information.** The wand carries the persistent exact reading. A peripheral prompt card holds one question or explanation at a time. Labels appear beside selected geometry and fade after release; the calculation instrument opens only when requested or at its teaching moment. Text uses readable Quest scale. Source, physical amber equipotentials, teal observation, and violet mathematical boundary differ in material and silhouette as well as color.
- **Student explanation.** After each pivotal observation, ask the learner to say an explanation in their own words and optionally save a short response through an accessible companion input or teacher worksheet. Do not pretend that selecting a multiple-choice answer proves causal understanding. Automatic speech recognition is optional, not a prerequisite for progress.
- **Teacher/learner control.** Offer Back, Repeat, Continue, Skip explanation, Show workbook connection, Reset apparatus, and Free Exploration. Skipping does not change the scene physics. Re-entering a phase restores its overlay preset but need not teleport the wand or origin; an explicit “restore demonstration pose” is separate and undoable.

### Example prompt and feedback language

| Moment | Student-facing prompt | First response when the prediction conflicts with observation | Explanation offered after a retry or on request |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distance | “Hold the tip twice as far from the charge. What should happen to the voltage?” | “Pin both readings. Which point was twice as far from the source?” | “For this point charge, potential varies as one over source–tip distance.” |
| Equal radius | “Try a different direction at the same distance. What will the wand report?” | “Keep the distance marker fixed and move around the charge once more.” | “All points that far from this one source have the same potential; together they form a sphere.” |
| Origin | “Slide only the violet grip. Which physical measurement should change?” | “Look at the charge and wand tip. Did either endpoint move?” | “Both coordinate vectors changed from their new starting point; their difference did not.” |
| Moments | “Put the origin above the charge. What sign should the first higher moment have?” | “Read the direction of the signed source-position arrow before opening the number.” | “The source lies below the chosen origin, so its signed axial coordinate and $Q_1=qz'$ are negative.” |
| Truncation | “Will adding the next term necessarily make the displayed sum identical to the exact reading?” | “Open the omitted-term slot and compare two exterior tip positions.” | “A finite sum omits later terms; the exact Coulomb reading is the independent reference.” |

## Phase-by-phase instructional storyboard

Each phase includes a short prediction, physical test, observation, explanation, and application. Suggested examples are cues rather than a script to recite verbatim.

### Phase 1 — Encounter the physical phenomenon

1. **Starting point and outcome.** Assume the student knows that a positive source can affect a nearby location, but may not know potential's distance law. Outcome: describe $V$ as a scalar reading at the wand tip and predict its dependence on source–tip separation.
2. **Visible scene.** One fixed $+q$ source and the docked wand. The exact tip reading is active. The origin carriage may be physically present but visually quiet; no moment panel, amber equipotential patches, or violet convergence boundary appears in Guided mode. No equation on entry.
3. **Quest action.** Grab the wand handle; sample a near point, a farther point along roughly the same direction, then a point at a different direction. Physical walking is optional. The tip, not the hand or headset, sets the measurement.
4. **Observation.** For $q>0$, readings are positive, decrease with distance, and match at equal physical distances within measurement display precision. Direction alone does not change $V$ for this source.
5. **Explanation.** The same point charge is the only physical source. Its scalar potential depends on how far the observation tip is from it. Moving the coordinate origin early does not alter this separation.
6. **Math, when useful.** After the near/far comparison, reveal $V_{\rm exact}\propto 1/\text{source–tip distance}$ and, if wanted, “positive $q$ divided by the physical distance, times the constant $k=1/(4\pi\varepsilon_0)$.” Introduce $\lvert\mathbf r-\mathbf r'\rvert$ only after the vectors have meaning in Phase 3. At the source itself, ideal point-charge $V$ is undefined.
7. **Prediction/check.** “If the tip's distance from $+q$ doubles, will this positive reading halve, double, or stay the same?” Follow with “If I go around the charge at the same distance, should the reading change?”
8. **Response to an incorrect prediction.** Invite the learner to pin two sampled readings and compare the source–tip distances; offer an equal-distance trial. Then ask which action changed distance. Reveal $1/\text{distance}$ only after the student has seen the relation or requests help.
9. **Advance/repeat/skip.** Continue is always available. A suggested readiness cue is one correct near/far prediction plus a spoken or written sentence linking the reading to source–tip distance. Repeat with new positions; skipping carries the live wand position forward.

### Phase 2 — Discover equipotential surfaces

1. **Starting point and outcome.** The student has seen a distance trend but may still expect directional variation. Outcome: infer that equal-distance points form a sphere of equal scalar potential and distinguish that sphere from an electric-field path.
2. **Visible scene.** Begin with charge, wand, and two optional saved measurement markers. The three amber spherical cutaways stay hidden until the student compares separated locations at equal source distance or selects “show the shape.” Reveal one patch first, then the other two, without a traveling pulse.
3. **Quest action.** Sample two points on different sides of the charge while keeping the tip at the same source distance; compare with a third point at a different radius. Walk or turn the wand to inspect the cutaway in three dimensions.
4. **Observation.** Equal source radius gives equal exact $V$, regardless of azimuth or polar direction. Different radii have different values. The amber patches are nested and remain centered on the physical charge when the student moves around them.
5. **Explanation.** A surface joining points with one scalar $V$ is an equipotential surface. For an isolated point charge, the distance rule makes these surfaces concentric spheres. The electric field is a **vector** directed outward for $+q$; a surface of constant $V$ is not a field line, flow of charge, or physical material.
6. **Math, when useful.** At each revealed radius $a_i$, show the local label $V_i=kq/a_i$ in volts. A compact optional note may relate field to the spatial change of potential, $\mathbf E=-\nabla V$, and for this case $|\mathbf E|=kq/a_i^2$. This distinction is included because students must not infer that equal potential means zero field; deriving field is outside Experiment 1's main task.
7. **Prediction/check.** “On the same amber shell but around the back, will the wand read a different voltage? Does equal voltage mean the field is zero?”
8. **Response to an incorrect prediction.** Suggest two positions on the same shell, let the student read both, and show outward direction arrows **only if field is being confused with potential**. Ask what stayed equal and what has direction. Do not label a wrong answer as a failure.
9. **Advance/repeat/skip.** Continue after the student identifies a shell as a set of equal readings and says why its center is the source. Repeat with another shell or hide/reveal again. “Show shape” and Skip remain available without a forced number of samples.

### Phase 3 — Understand the coordinate origin

1. **Starting point and outcome.** The student knows the exact physical pattern. Outcome: separate the fixed source and tip from the movable reference point $O$, and explain which lengths and vectors are coordinate-dependent.
2. **Visible scene.** Brighten the origin rail and grip. Keep the amber shells fixed. When the origin is selected, show slender $O\to\text{source}$ and $O\to\text{tip}$ arrows with labels $\mathbf r'$ and $\mathbf r$. A third short source-to-tip segment may be requested for direct comparison. Do **not** introduce the convergence sphere yet.
3. **Quest action.** Leave the wand tip parked or held still, predict the exact reading, then slide $O$ up and down its rail, including past the source if reachable. Pause at source alignment. The axial rail makes the lesson's $+z$ reference explicit.
4. **Observation.** The charge, tip, physical separation, exact wand value, and amber shells do not move. The two vectors from $O$, their lengths, and their component values move with it. $\mathbf r-\mathbf r'$ stays the same physical source-to-tip displacement.
5. **Explanation.** $O$ is where the coordinates start, not a second source. A prime marks a **source coordinate**, not a derivative. The observation coordinate is measured from the same $O$. Moving $O$ changes both coordinate descriptions but cancels in their difference.
6. **Math, when useful.** Introduce $\mathbf r'=\mathbf x_{\rm source}-\mathbf x_O$, $\mathbf r=\mathbf x_{\rm tip}-\mathbf x_O$, and $\mathbf r-\mathbf r'=\mathbf x_{\rm tip}-\mathbf x_{\rm source}$. The $\mathbf x$ expressions are a brief vector-subtraction aid; the displayed lesson notation remains $\mathbf r',\mathbf r$. Then point back to $V_{\rm exact}=kq/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$. The lecture's polar $\theta$ is measured from $+z$, represented by world vertical in VR; $\phi$ is azimuth. $r,r'$ are magnitudes, not vectors.
7. **Prediction/check.** “If only the violet $O$ moves, which of these changes: the wand reading, $\mathbf r'$, $\mathbf r$, the amber surfaces?” Require a brief reason, not merely selected tiles.
8. **Response to an incorrect prediction.** Offer a before/after freeze frame with the source and tip visually registered; highlight the common starting point of both vectors and the unchanged source-to-tip segment. Ask the student to try a larger origin displacement before revealing the algebraic cancellation.
9. **Advance/repeat/skip.** Continue after the student can identify all three physical roles and explain why the exact reading stayed fixed. Let them replay the origin motion or choose a different stationary tip location. Skip leaves the origin and wand manipulable.

### Phase 4 — Discover the multipole representation

1. **Starting point and outcome.** The student distinguishes physical and coordinate geometry, but has not been asked to use a series. Outcome: understand why an ordered exterior representation is useful for source distributions, derive the centered point-charge case, and interpret origin-dependent terms without treating them as extra physical fields.
2. **Visible scene.** Open a compact optional calculation instrument beside the exact wand reference. First show a single monopole contribution. On request, reveal the source-position weight and then higher $Q_n$, one row at a time. Keep moment values with their own units; compare **potential contributions $V_n$** on a common volts scale.
3. **Quest action.** Place $O$ at the charge and inspect $Q_0$ and higher rows; then slide $O$ below and above while the tip stays still. At an exterior tip location, enable $n=0,1,2,3$ successively and compare each truncated sum with exact $V$. Let the student reposition the tip to a farther exterior location. If the tip moves outside the calculation's valid region before Phase 5, keep exact $V$ active, close the sum, and offer “Why did the calculation close?” as the transition into Phase 5.
4. **Observation.** Centered: $Q_0=q$, all $Q_{n\ge1}=0$, and the monopole equals exact potential for $r>0$. Off-center: higher moments appear, odd ones reverse sign when the signed source coordinate $z'$ reverses; exact $V$ stays fixed if the tip stays fixed. Each finite sum may differ from exact $V$; farther exterior samples generally need less correction.
5. **Explanation.** For complex distributions, the multipole method orders the distant potential by powers of $1/r$, allowing the leading source geometry to be read from the first surviving terms. One point charge is the simplest test: the same physical Coulomb potential can have a one-term centered description or a longer off-center description. The terms are **parts of a calculation**, not separate charges or independently measured potentials.
6. **Math, when useful.** Introduce the guide's moment integral $Q_n=\int r'^nP_n(\cos\theta')\,dQ$: $dQ$ is a piece of **physical charge** (the whole $q$ for this one-point source), $n$ labels an angular order, and $P_n$ is the Legendre angular pattern used by the lesson. The $Q_n$ are weighted descriptors of that charge, not additional charge. For one axial $+q$, $Q_n=q(z')^n$, with $z'=z_{\rm source}-z_O$, $Q_0=q$, $Q_1=qz'$, $Q_2=q(z')^2$. On the positive or negative $z$ axis, $P_n(\pm1)=(\pm1)^n$, which makes $r'^nP_n(\cos\theta')=(z')^n$. At $z'=0$, $Q_{n\ge1}=0$; the optional Workbook 2b explanation uses $|r'^nP_n|\le r'^n\to0$, so the undefined $\theta'$ is harmless. Next show $V_n=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q_n}{r^{n+1}}P_n(\cos\theta)$ and $V^{(N)}=\sum_{n=0}^N V_n$. $P_0=1$, $P_1(x)=x$, and $P_2(x)=(3x^2-1)/2$ appear only as their terms are opened. Explain the reason for the series with the guide's Coulomb-kernel expansion $1/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|=\sum_{n\ge0}(r'^n/r^{n+1})P_n(\cos\gamma)$ for $r>r'$; for an axial source the angular relation produces the displayed $P_n(\cos\theta)$ form. Full algebra is optional workbook support.
7. **Prediction/check.** “When $O$ sits on $+q$, which higher moments survive? If $O$ passes above the charge, what happens to the sign of $Q_1$? If the n=0 estimate changes while exact $V$ does not, which quantity changed?”
8. **Response to an incorrect prediction.** Hide the number, show the signed $z'$ arrow and ask for its sign; reveal $Q_1=qz'$ only after the geometric prediction. If the student claims higher moments are new physical sources, invite a fixed-tip exact reading comparison. If they assume a finite sum is exact, show the omitted-term slot and compare at two exterior distances.
9. **Advance/repeat/skip.** Continue after the student explains one centered and one off-center description of the **same** charge. Reopen individual rows or the kernel explanation as needed. Higher-order rows are optional and never required to move the apparatus.

### Phase 5 — Investigate convergence

1. **Starting point and outcome.** The student has seen a finite exterior approximation. Outcome: explain why its stated guarantee depends on the chosen origin and why exact potential persists when that guarantee is absent.
2. **Visible scene.** Now reveal the violet origin-centered boundary as a sparse, mathematical sphere or meridian with a different silhouette/material from amber equipotentials. Its radius is labeled $R_{\max}$ only while studied. The exact wand stays active; the approximation instrument can show terms outside and a clearly closed “exterior result unavailable here” state at or inside.
3. **Quest action.** Set $O$ away from the charge and watch the boundary touch the charge. Predict which side permits the exterior calculation. Move the **tip** across the boundary and back, including a point inside the violet boundary but away from the physical source. Move $O$ again while the tip stays fixed to see the domain change.
4. **Observation.** $R_{\max}=r'$ for this one source, so the violet boundary follows $O$ and changes radius. The exact potential responds only to tip–source separation, not to boundary crossing alone. The truncated exterior display is available only when $r>R_{\max}$; it closes at $r\le R_{\max}$.
5. **Explanation.** The guide expands the Coulomb denominator in the ratio of source radius to observation radius. The exterior guarantee requires that ratio be below one for **every source element**. For one charge, the greatest source radius is its distance from $O$. The violet surface states a mathematical condition, not a physical equipotential or forbidden place to move. At or inside, this exterior formula has no general guarantee; that does **not** say exact $V$ is zero or undefined. Exact $V$ is undefined only at the ideal point charge.
6. **Math, when useful.** Introduce $R_{\max}=\max_{\rm source}r'=|\mathbf r'|$ and $r>R_{\max}$. Show the ratio $r'/r<1$ at the selected tip, linked to the two rays. State that an infinite series can equal exact $V$ in the valid region, while $V^{(N)}$ is a truncation with omitted terms. When $O$ is at the charge, $R_{\max}=0$ and the centered monopole gives the exact potential for every $r>0$.
7. **Prediction/check.** “The tip is inside violet but not at $+q$: is the physical potential zero, undefined, or still measurable? Which mathematical result can we promise here?”
8. **Response to an incorrect prediction.** Keep the exact meter visibly live, let the student move the tip across violet without touching the source, and compare the continuous exact readings. Point to the boundary's center $O$ and the amber shells' center at $+q$. Ask the learner to restate what each marker represents before showing the ratio explanation.
9. **Advance/repeat/skip.** Continue after the student identifies the exterior region and distinguishes exact measurement from the withheld approximation. Repeat by changing $O$, which moves the mathematical boundary while leaving the physical shells fixed. Skipping never blocks wand travel.

### Phase 6 — Independent investigation and transfer

1. **Starting point and outcome.** The student has encountered all ingredients. Outcome: use them together to make and defend a new prediction, not repeat the demonstrated lower-origin motion.
2. **Visible scene.** Offer an optional “mystery configuration” card: $O$ starts **above** $+q$, and the wand tip starts at an unfamiliar off-axis exterior location. No explanatory arrows, term bars, or boundary are pre-opened; the exact meter remains active. The student can reveal any overlay deliberately. The same source and engine are used; no new charge distribution is implied.
3. **Quest action.** Before moving, predict the sign of $Q_1$, whether exact $V$ changes if only $O$ moves, and whether the current tip meets the exterior condition. Test those predictions. Then choose two different tip positions at the same distance from $+q$, and choose one tip position that is inside the violet boundary but away from the charge.
4. **Observation.** The student should find negative $z'$ and $Q_1$ when $O$ is above the source, equal exact $V$ on equal-radius positions, origin-dependent $r,r',Q_n,R_{\max}$, and an exact reading despite a withheld exterior sum inside violet.
5. **Explanation.** Ask for a short own-words account with three claims and evidence: what is physical and invariant, what depends on the coordinate choice, and when an exterior truncation is meaningful. A correct account names source–tip distance and the $r>R_{\max}$ condition.
6. **Math, when useful.** The student selects only equations needed to justify the answer: $V_{\rm exact}=kq/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, $Q_1=qz'$, $V^{(N)}=\sum_{n=0}^N V_n$, and $r>R_{\max}$. An optional Workbook handoff asks them to compare their one-charge reasoning with Activity 3's **two-charge** setup, while making clear that new charge geometry is not simulated here.
7. **Prediction/check.** “Move $O$ through the charge while keeping the tip fixed. Which sign changes, which measured voltage does not, and how will you decide whether the exterior sum may be shown?” Require an explanation before optional reveal.
8. **Response to an incorrect prediction.** Give a hint ladder: (1) highlight source and tip only; (2) show the signed source vector $z'$; (3) show $O$-to-tip radius beside $O$-to-source radius; (4) offer the relevant equation. Preserve the student's first prediction so they can explain the revision.
9. **Advance/repeat/skip.** This is the final application, not a pass/fail gate. The learner can submit an own-words response, retry with another configuration, or leave for the Workbook. Free Exploration remains available throughout.

## Physical and display specifications

| Object/display | Behavior across phases |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed $+q$ and sensing wand | Always physical and operable. The tip is the exact observation point. The exact potential appears on the wand in both modes and all phases, except “undefined at charge” if the tip coincides with the ideal source. |
| Origin rail and grip | Always operable, constrained to world vertical = lesson $z$; in Guided Phases 1–2 it is visually quiet and its explanatory labels wait until Phase 3. Early movement prompts an optional explanation, never a lock or reset. |
| Amber spherical equipotentials | Hidden for Guided Phase 1; revealed from observed equal-radius measurements in Phase 2. Source-centered and stationary in every later phase. Their displayed $V_i=kq/a_i$ values are exact, not separate sources. |
| Source and observation vectors | Temporary on selection from Phase 3; $\mathbf r'$ ends at the charge and $\mathbf r$ ends at the wand **tip**. Show $\mathbf r-\mathbf r'$ only when comparing the invariant physical separation. |
| Moment and contribution instrument | Introduced in Phase 4. Label $Q_n$ with $\mathrm{C}\,\mathrm{m}^{n}$ or $\mathrm{nC}\,\mathrm{m}^{n}$. Plot $V_n$, signed and in volts, with exact $V$ as a separate reference. An order control changes $N$ without changing the source or exact meter. |
| Violet convergence marker | Hidden in Guided Phases 1–4, introduced in Phase 5. Centered on $O$ with radius $R_{\max}$; distinguish it by dashed/segmented line, no amber fill, and a domain label. It never blocks grabbing or walking. |

**Guided mode** controls reveal timing and offers questions, hints, workbook links, and optional explanation capture. **Free Exploration** immediately allows any overlay to be toggled on or off while retaining the same charge, wand, origin constraint, exact physics, and exterior-domain rule. Switching modes preserves object poses and measurements. “Reveal” changes presentation only.

## Evidence of learning and evaluation

An interaction log can record predictions, sampled tip positions, origin position, requested hints, and revisions; a teacher or student can use the following rubric without requiring speech recognition. Merely moving an object or choosing a correct tile does not meet the criterion.

| Understanding | Observable evidence for a satisfactory explanation | Diagnostic misconception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance law | Predicts that doubling source–tip distance halves positive point-charge $V$; cites the denominator in $kq/\lvert\mathbf r-\mathbf r'\rvert$. | Treats the wand handle or headset position as the measurement point; predicts inverse-square potential. |
| Equal-distance potential | Samples two different directions at the same radius and explains the equal $V$ using spherical symmetry; distinguishes scalar $V$ from vector $\mathbf E$. | Calls an amber shell a field line or says field vanishes along it. |
| Physical invariance under origin shift | Holds source and tip fixed, predicts unchanged exact $V$, and identifies unchanged source–tip separation as the cause. | Calls $O$ another charge or expects exact $V$ to track $r$ alone. |
| Origin-dependent moments | Predicts $Q_0=q$, centered higher moments zero, and sign reversal of $Q_1=qz'$ above/below source; states that moments describe the same physical charge. | Interprets nonzero $Q_1$ as a new physical dipole source. |
| Truncation versus exact | Correctly identifies $V^{(N)}$ as a partial sum and exact $V$ as the independent Coulomb measurement; explains an observed difference by omitted terms. | Assumes a finite sum must always match exact $V$, or compares $Q_n$ with different units as equivalent bars. |
| Exterior condition | Uses $r>R_{\max}$ with distances from the **same** $O$, predicts the available side of the boundary, and says exact $V$ remains defined away from $+q$ when the exterior display closes. | Calls the violet marker an equipotential, a physical wall, or the locus where $V=0$. |

**Transfer check:** Without opening overlays, present $O$ above the charge and an off-axis tip. Ask the learner to predict the sign of $Q_1$, whether the exact reading changes if only $O$ moves, and whether the exterior sum can be used after moving the tip to a specified inside-violet point. They must justify each with a distinct geometric relationship. Success requires all three causal reasons; an answer that only reports numbers is insufficient. For a written extension, Workbook Activity 2c asks what changes when the coordinate origin moves, and Activity 1a asks the student to name the source and observation variables.

### Decisions to resolve before implementation

1. **Evidence capture:** Is a spoken explanation enough for a live instructor, or should the product collect a typed/recorded response? If collected, specify consent, storage, and review workflow. The experience itself must function without recording.
2. **Phase 2 reveal trigger:** Prefer an evidence-based prompt after two equal-radius readings, with an always-available “show shape” control. Decide how approximate “equal radius” should be at normal hand-tracking precision; no exact-position gate.
3. **Truncation control:** Stage 1 computes through $n=3$. Confirm whether the Phase 4 learner may vary $N$ beyond 3 and what practical cap keeps the Quest display clear. The physics formula supports higher orders, but performance and interpretation need headset review.
4. **Boundary language:** Use “exterior expansion not guaranteed here” at $r\le R_{\max}$. Do not claim the infinite series necessarily diverges at every such point. Maintain the exact point-charge singularity only at the source.
5. **Reference convention:** Keep the guide's $\theta$ from $+z$, $\phi$ azimuth, with world vertical mapped to $z$. The workbook notes this is opposite the learner's preferred symbol naming; make the convention visible once and stable thereafter.

## Review questions for a student pilot

After each phase, ask the learner for a short causal explanation before showing the next formula. At the end, can they accurately say **what is physical**, **what is a chosen coordinate description**, **what the finite sum approximates**, and **where the exterior representation has a guarantee**? Observe whether the student can complete the transfer check without repeatedly opening the explanatory overlays. Use those responses, not time spent or number of grabs, to decide whether the sequence works.
